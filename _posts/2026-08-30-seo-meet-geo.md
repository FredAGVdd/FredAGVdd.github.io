I've done SEO on and off for the best part of two decades. The rules of thumb were stable for a long time: structure your content, mind your metadata, earn your links, keep it fast.

Then people started *asking the AI* instead of scrolling a results page — and a new question landed on my desk: not just "does this rank?" but "does this get **quoted** by the answer engine?" That's GEO — Generative Engine Optimization — and it's SEO's slightly weird younger sibling.

A lot of the fundamentals carry straight over: clean structure, good structured data, unambiguous facts. But the emphasis shifts. Answer engines love content that's **quotable in isolation** — a clear claim with its context attached, not a claim that only makes sense after three paragraphs of preamble. Semantic clarity beats keyword density. Being the source that's easy to lift wins.

Is it a fully-formed discipline yet? No. It's early, and half the "best practices" out there are guesses. But it's real, it's already affecting traffic, and pretending it's just SEO with a new hat is a mistake.

Structuring content so *both* a crawler and a model come away with the right answer — that's the game now.
