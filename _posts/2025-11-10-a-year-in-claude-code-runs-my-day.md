So. AI. I resisted the hype for a while — I've been writing software since the late nineties and I've seen a lot of "this changes everything" come and go. This one, though, actually changed my day.

For about a year now I've been building with Claude Code as a proper part of my workflow, not a party trick. The honest version: it's not that it writes the code *for* me — it's that the loop from "idea in ticket" to "reviewable pull request" got dramatically shorter. I design the thing, it drafts, I review hard, we iterate, and every change still goes through tests, linting and a full production build before it's allowed anywhere near `main`.

The part that surprised me is how much *senior* judgement matters more now, not less. Someone has to know what good looks like, spot the plausible-but-wrong answer, and keep the architecture honest. Twenty-five years of scar tissue turns out to be useful for exactly that.

More soon on the actual things I shipped with it. Some of it I'm quite proud of.
